Near-Term Quantum Computing

MSc Course

🏛 University of Amsterdam 📅 2023 – 2024 👥 Co-designed & co-taught

Near-term quantum computing sits at the boundary between what today's imperfect quantum hardware can do and the algorithms researchers are building to use it. This MSc course gave students a rigorous, hands-on introduction to the full stack — from the physical constraints of real devices to the algorithmic ideas designed to work within them.

The course opened with the fundamentals of near-term quantum hardware: how qubits are realised physically, what native gates are (the small set of operations a specific device can execute directly), and how circuit compilation maps abstract algorithms onto those native gates. Students learned why real devices are noisy — the sources of gate errors, decoherence, and measurement errors — and why this makes near-term computation qualitatively different from the fault-tolerant ideal.

A substantial portion of the course was dedicated to error suppression and mitigation: techniques such as dynamical decoupling, zero-noise extrapolation, and probabilistic error cancellation that allow useful results to be extracted from noisy hardware without full fault tolerance. Students worked through both the theory and practical implementation of these methods.

The algorithmic core focused on variational quantum algorithms — particularly the Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) and the Quantum Approximate Optimisation Algorithm (QAOA). These hybrid classical-quantum methods are among the most promising approaches for near-term devices. Students learned how ansatz design, classical optimisation, and hardware constraints interact, and where these algorithms can offer genuine advantage.

Throughout, lectures were paired with hands-on programming exercises using quantum computing frameworks, giving students direct experience writing, compiling, and running circuits.

Course topics at a glance:

  • Quantum hardware and qubit technologies
  • Native gates and circuit compilation
  • Noise models: gate errors, decoherence, readout errors
  • Error suppression (dynamical decoupling)
  • Error mitigation (ZNE, PEC, readout correction)
  • Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE)
  • Quantum Approximate Optimisation (QAOA)
  • Ansatz design and expressibility
  • Classical optimisers for variational algorithms
  • Hands-on exercises with real quantum frameworks

The course concluded with a three-week project in which student teams independently implemented a variational quantum algorithm of their choice, ran it on real IBM Quantum hardware, and presented their findings — including an honest analysis of the effect of noise on their results and the mitigation strategies they applied.

Teaching Qualification: Obtained BKO/UTQ (Basiskwalificatie Onderwijs / University Teaching Qualification) from the University of Amsterdam.
